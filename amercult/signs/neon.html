Light and commercial identity

Neon signs bring lettering and illustration into the night through shaped, illuminated glass tubing. The outline of a word or image becomes a source of light, giving a business an identity that can look very different after dark.

The American Sign Museum preserves this tradition alongside other forms of commercial signage. Its working neon shop also demonstrates that signs are crafted objects: making and maintaining them requires specialized skills. Looking closely at a sign means noticing the glass contours, supporting structure, and relationship between the luminous design and its setting.

What to look for

  • Letterforms traced by glass tubing
  • A design that changes between day and night
  • Specialized fabrication and repair skills
