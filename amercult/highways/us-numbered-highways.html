U.S. Numbered Highways

Home / American Culture / Highways / U.S. Numbered Highways

Established in 1926

Before a coordinated numbering system, motorists often navigated long-distance roads by following named trails and their distinctive markers. The U.S. numbered highway system, approved by the American Association of State Highway Officials in November 1926, offered a more consistent way to identify routes.

These route numbers organized travel across a network of roads rather than creating a single new roadway. Their familiar shields became part of town streets and rural intersections alike. The system helps explain why a highway can be both a long-distance connection and a community's main commercial street.

What to look for

  • Numbers used across state boundaries
  • A recognizable shield-shaped marker
  • Routes passing through established communities
