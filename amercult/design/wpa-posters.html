WPA Travel Posters

Home / American Culture / Design / WPA Travel Posters

New Deal graphic design

During the New Deal, artists working in Works Progress Administration programs produced posters for a wide range of public purposes. The Library of Congress collection includes examples promoting travel, parks, exhibitions, and community activities from 1936 to 1943.

Travel posters distilled a landscape into a few memorable shapes and a short message. Broad areas of color, simplified scenery, and prominent lettering made them effective at a distance. Their lasting appeal also makes them useful evidence of how public agencies presented recreation and the American landscape to potential visitors.

What to look for

  • Simplified landscapes and bold silhouettes
  • Limited, clearly separated color areas
  • Short messages with prominent lettering
