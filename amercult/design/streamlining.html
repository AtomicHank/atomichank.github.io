American furniture of the 1930s

Streamlined furniture brought the visual language of modernity into interiors. Horizontal emphasis, curved edges, and reflective surfaces could make a desk feel related to the wider world of modern architecture and manufactured goods.

A Paul Frankl desk discussed by Cooper Hewitt illustrates this approach. Parallel lines, colored edges, chrome elements, and curved shelves organize a practical object into a clear composition. Frankl also connected modern design with mass production and consumer choice. The example shows that streamlining in furniture was a matter of visual form and contemporary identity, rather than an aerodynamic requirement.

What to look for

  • Strong horizontal lines
  • Curved accents and reflective metal
  • Color used to organize a composition
