P-51 Mustang

Home / Aviation / Military Aviation / P-51 Mustang

World War II · Fighter escort

The North American P-51 Mustang became especially important as a long-range escort fighter during World War II. Fitting the design with the Merlin engine improved high-altitude performance, helping turn the Mustang into an effective partner for American heavy bombers operating over Europe.

The P-51D combined that performance with a bubble canopy and six wing-mounted machine guns. The aircraft also served in ground-attack roles and continued into the postwar period under the F-51 designation. Its history shows how an airframe's usefulness depends on the combination of engine, range, visibility, and mission requirements.

What to look for

  • A single liquid-cooled engine with a streamlined nose
  • A radiator intake beneath the fuselage
  • A bubble canopy on the P-51D
