# Regimental battalion page research — September 2026

Reviewed the existing infantry and field artillery battalion collections. Added 120 historical entries to 76 pages: 62 infantry/Ranger entries on 37 pages and 58 field artillery entries on 39 pages. The other 180 battalion pages, including the previously expanded 1st Infantry page, are unchanged. Parent-page link descriptions were updated only for expanded pages.

Each added card links to its evidence. Entries summarize documented periods of service rather than claiming to provide complete lineage certificates or a current order of battle. The original cards remain intact. Regimental illustrations are explicitly labeled where a battalion-specific illustration is unavailable.

Field artillery additions follow Janice E. McKenney, *Field Artillery* (U.S. Army Center of Military History, 2010), [Part 1](https://d34w7g4gy10iej.cloudfront.net/pubs/pdf_71908.pdf) and [Part 2](https://d34w7g4gy10iej.cloudfront.net/pubs/pdf_71903.pdf), whose published lineages principally extend through 2003. Individual cards link to the relevant PDF page. Successive battalion designations and later battery lineages are explained in the copy. Inactive headquarters designations without a documented battalion activation were not used to invent additional fielded units.

Infantry research used Army lineage documents and publications, regimental and divisional associations, the Army Historical Foundation, and secondary histories identified on the cards. The [National Guard battalion survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_infantry_battalions_of_the_Army_National_Guard_from_1959) provided a cross-check for the Guard regiments. A page left unchanged means this review did not establish an additional battalion; it is not proof that no provisional or short-lived unit ever existed.

The 75th page includes the separate 4th–6th Ranger Infantry Battalions as explicitly labeled lineage predecessors, plus the Regimental Special Troops and Regimental Military Intelligence Battalions. The support battalions have their proper names, without invented battalion numbers.
